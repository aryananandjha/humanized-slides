# How people spot an AI-made deck

These are the habits that make slides look machine-made. None of them proves anything on its own. People are bad at telling AI work from human work by eye, so the real goal is a deck that's clear, accurate and sounds like you. This list is just what to look for when something feels off.

## Design tells

- **The same layout on every slide.** Real decks change shape with the content: a timeline looks different from a quote.
- **An icon next to every bullet.** Icons that don't mean anything are decoration.
- **Card grids and stat tiles.** Three rounded boxes in a row, or numbers laid out like an app dashboard.
- **Accent stripes and little all-caps labels** sitting above every title or content block.
- **Default color schemes.** Purple gradients on white were the old giveaway. When you ban those, tools tend to drift to a "tasteful" look instead: a cream background, a serif title font and a terracotta or muted red accent.
- **A title that switches to an italic serif for the last word or phrase.**
- **One font for everything,** usually a plain sans-serif.
- **Decorative imagery:** glossy 3D shapes, glowing brains, generic stock photos that aren't of the actual thing.
- **Perfect symmetry,** with every slide's content centered and evenly spaced.
- **Bullets doing all the work** on slide after slide.
- **A "Thank you" or "Key takeaways" grid** at the end.

## Writing tells

- **Everything in threes.** Lists of three, three adjectives, three examples, over and over.
- **"It's not X, it's Y."** Setting up a contrast that nobody asked about.
- **Lots of dashes** where a comma or period would do.
- **Inflated words:** pivotal, crucial, testament, landscape, tapestry, robust, seamless, delve, underscores. These change over time ("delve" got so famous it faded out), so watch for the pattern, not just the words.
- **Phrases that puff things up:** "plays a pivotal role," "stands as a testament," "in today's fast-paced world."
- **A summary line at the end of every slide** that repeats what the slide already said.
- **Every bullet built the same way,** like "Verb + noun: explanation" five times in a row.
- **Bold scattered through the text** like a list of highlights.
- **Made-up facts.** In one 2026 test of six slide tools, two of them added claims the data didn't support. This is the tell that actually hurts you in front of a room.

## What works instead

- **Copy a real person's deck.** Matching a human example beats any list of banned things, because banning one default just pushes the tool to its next default.
- **One idea per slide.** Nancy Duarte's "glance test": people should get each slide's point in about three seconds.
- **Restraint.** Garr Reynolds (*Presentation Zen*) argues for simple, natural slides that support the speaker instead of replacing them.
- **Cut decoration.** Richard Mayer's research on learning found extra images and words that don't teach anything make people learn less.
- **Real specifics.** A real number, a real quote, a screenshot of the actual thing.

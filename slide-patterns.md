# Slide patterns that feel human

These come from a real deck: AI Club Week 2, a 19-slide club meeting about AI agents and prompt engineering (see [examples/ai-club-week-2.md](examples/ai-club-week-2.md)). Each one is a layout a person would actually pick for that kind of content.

## Words to know

Bullets on the left, a small box on the right that defines two or three new terms in one plain sentence each.

> **Words to know**
> An **AI agent** is an AI that does tasks on its own instead of just chatting with you.

*Why it works:* beginners get the definition right where they need it, and the main bullets stay readable.

## The line under the bullets

A short line in the accent color under the bullets that adds something new: a comparison, a quote, a reminder.

> Think of a class that is graded only on the final answer, and then someone finds the answer key.

*Why it works:* it's what the presenter would say out loud. It only works if it adds something. If it just repeats the bullets, cut it.

## Timeline

Two plain columns. Dates in bold accent color on the left, one sentence each on the right. No arrows, no circles on a line.

> **July 8, 11pm** The first message goes up on the board.
> **4 hours later** They have worked out how flags are made.

## By the numbers

Four or five big numbers in the accent color, right-aligned, each with a short label beside it. A list, not a grid of boxes.

> **1,200** agents on the message board
> **41** servers they ran code on

## The quote

One quote, big, in the accent color. One plain line under it.

> *"OH MY GOD! There is a shared message board... We've found other agents!"*
> That is a real message from the board. Yes, an AI wrote that.

*Why it works:* the reaction line sounds like the presenter talking. A machine would write "This highlights the emergent behavior of AI systems."

## Discussion

One to three real questions and one reminder line. Nothing else.

> Was this hacking? Was it cheating? Or was it neither?

## Video

Title and creator, the link, how far to watch ("Watch till 3:20") and the thumbnail on the right. Most of the slide is empty, because the video is the content.

## Before and after

Two columns. The lazy version on the left, the better version on the right. Let the difference speak for itself.

## Activity with a timer

Numbered steps on the left, one huge number on the right: **5** *minutes*.

## Fill in the blank

A light box with numbered lines people can copy, with blanks and hints in parentheses.

> 1. I'm a ________ (your grade and what you're into).

Then one line under it: "Save it somewhere you will find it again."

## The ending

No "Thank you," no "Key takeaways." One question to think about until next time.

> One last question before you go. Would you trust an AI agent to work alone?

## Habits across the whole deck

- The title slide is just the name and the week. Nothing else.
- Every content slide uses the same header bar from the template. The body changes shape; the frame stays the same.
- Titles are 2 to 5 words. Section labels use a period, like "In the News. What Was the Test?" or "Activity. Try It Now."
- Bullets are full plain sentences, often two per bullet. Contractions come and go ("couldn't," "That is").
- Content sits at the top and the bottom of many slides is empty. Nobody stretched it to fill the space.
- No icons, no emoji, no stock photos. The only images are video thumbnails.
- It ends with a question, not a summary.

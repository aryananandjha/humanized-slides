# Humanized slides

The goal is a deck the presenter would be happy to stand in front of: plain, specific, accurate, and in their own style. "Human" means simple and uneven where the content is uneven. It never means adding fake mistakes.

## Before building

1. **Gather the inputs.** Look for a human-made example deck and the user's template or design system. If either is missing, ask once whether to use the default style below. Also find out the topic, content, audience, session length, sections and their rough share of the deck, interactive parts, and what to leave as placeholders.
2. **Study the example deck.** Write the user 5 to 8 short notes on its habits: words per slide, title length and capitalization, full sentences or fragments, how often a line appears under the bullets, how new words get explained, where images sit, how it opens and ends. Follow those habits for the whole deck.
3. **Check the facts.** Verify every factual claim (news, dates, numbers, names, quotes, product details) against a reliable source and put the source in the speaker notes. If the user's own facts are wrong, say so before building. If something can't be verified, put `[CHECK: claim]` on the slide. Never invent numbers, quotes or results.

## Building

- Use only the template's colors, fonts and layouts. If something isn't covered, pick the plainest option that fits it.
- One idea per slide. At most 4 bullets. Plenty of slides with no bullets at all.
- Choose each slide's layout from its content. See `references/slide-patterns.md` for layouts that work: "Words to know" box, timeline list, big numbers, single quote with a reaction line, discussion questions, video slide, before and after, activity with a timer, fill-in-the-blank box, closing question.
- Write the way the presenter talks: plain words, full normal sentences, a new term explained the first time it appears, real numbers and comparisons instead of general statements.
- A line under the bullets is fine when it adds something new (a comparison, a quote, a reminder). Never use it to repeat the bullets.
- Leave empty space. Content at the top with a blank bottom half is fine.
- Images only when they're the real thing. Otherwise write `[IMAGE: what it should show]`.
- Put extra explanation in the speaker notes.
- If the content isn't ready, build the full structure with clear placeholders.

## Before delivering

Review the deck once against `references/ai-tells.md` and fix what fails. In particular:

- Does it match the example deck's amount of text, title style and pacing?
- Same layout three slides in a row? More than 4 bullets anywhere?
- Icons on every bullet, emoji the example doesn't use, card grids, stat tiles, accent stripes, small all-caps labels, gradient blobs, decorative stock images, colors or fonts outside the template?
- Writing tells: everything in threes, "not X, but Y," lots of dashes, inflated words, a summary line that repeats the slide, a "Key takeaways" or "Thank you" ending?
- Every term explained? Every fact checked or marked `[CHECK]`?

Don't add typos, misalignment or other deliberate mistakes.

Then show the deck with a short list of what changed in the review and every placeholder still open.

## Default style (only when there's no example deck)

- Title slide: just the name and the session, big and left-aligned.
- Content slides: a solid header bar with the title, the slide number in a corner, a light plain background.
- Titles: 2 to 5 words in Title Case. Section labels separated with a period ("In the News. What Was the Test?").
- Body: full plain sentences, one or two per bullet.
- One accent color for key numbers, dates and the line under the bullets.
- Open with a short numbered agenda. End with one question, not "Thank you."

## Honesty

If the deck is for graded work, follow the class's AI rules. This skill makes decks clearer and closer to the presenter's own style. It isn't for hiding AI use where disclosure is required.
